01 — Overview

Research

My research sits at the interface of condensed matter theory and computational physics, with a focus on topological insulators, magnetic heterostructures, and spin-dependent transport. I investigate how interfacial symmetry breaking — driven by proximity to magnetic insulators or altermagnets — gives rise to non-trivial Berry phase topology, anisotropic Hall responses, and enhanced spin–charge interconversion.

Using a combination of density functional theory (DFT), Wannier tight-binding models, and low-energy effective Hamiltonians, I study systems including Bi₂Se₃/EuS and altermagnet–topological insulator bilayers. I also work on Monte Carlo simulations of spin systems and develop model Hamiltonians for bulk, slab, bilayer, and trilayer geometries.

My work is motivated by the broader goal of understanding and controlling quantum degrees of freedom at interfaces — knowledge central to designing next-generation spintronic and topological quantum devices.
